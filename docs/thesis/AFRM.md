# Affirm Holdings (AFRM)

**Tier:** Growth
**Accounts:** Schwab 6196 (351 shares)
**Cost Basis:** $25,779 (~$73.44/share)
**Last Updated:** 2026-03-06

## Thesis

BNPL infrastructure play — merchant adoption growing, becoming the default checkout financing option.

Affirm is building the rails for point-of-sale lending. Unlike legacy BNPL (Afterpay, Klarna) that offer simple pay-in-4, Affirm underwrites real credit risk with proprietary models and offers flexible terms (0% and interest-bearing). The merchant integration is the moat — once Affirm is embedded in checkout (Amazon, Shopify, Walmart), switching is painful.

Key pillars:
- **Merchant network:** Integrated with Amazon, Shopify, Walmart, Target — the largest e-commerce platforms. Network effects: more merchants → more consumers → more merchants
- **Underwriting edge:** Proprietary ML models with years of repayment data. Can price risk better than banks or simple BNPL competitors
- **Debit+ / Affirm Card:** Expanding from online checkout to everyday spending. If the card scales, TAM expands dramatically
- **Revenue model diversification:** Not just merchant fees — interest income, gain-on-sale from loan securitization, platform fees
- **Secular trend:** Gen-Z/millennial preference for transparent installment payments over revolving credit cards

## Invalidation Framework

### Early Warning
- Delinquency rates rising above underwriting targets for 2+ consecutive quarters
- GMV growth decelerating below 20%
- Amazon or Shopify renegotiate terms unfavorably or add competing BNPL options
- Affirm Card adoption stalls

### Invalidation Conditions
- **Credit losses blow up** — if charge-off rates spike in a recession and Affirm's underwriting proves no better than competitors, the edge disappears
- **Regulation kills unit economics** — if CFPB or state regulators cap fees/rates on BNPL products, the business model breaks
- **Merchant concentration risk** — if Amazon (largest partner) cuts the relationship or demands significantly worse economics
- **Funding market dries up** — Affirm needs capital markets to securitize loans. If ABS spreads blow out, they can't originate profitably

### What Would Make Me Add
- Affirm Card active users cross 2M+ (proves everyday spending use case)
- Stock pulls back to $65-70 on macro fear (not credit deterioration)
- GMV growth reaccelerates above 30%
- New major merchant partnership (e.g., Costco, Home Depot)

## Risk Notes

- **Single-product company** — unlike SOFI which has banking, investing, lending, and platform revenue, AFRM is essentially all BNPL. If BNPL as a category faces headwinds (regulation, credit losses, competition), there's no other business to fall back on
- 351 shares at ~$73 — mid-size position in Schwab 6196
- Consumer credit risk: recession directly impacts repayment rates
- Competitive pressure from Klarna (IPO'd), Apple Pay Later, bank BNPL products
- Path to GAAP profitability still in progress — need to see sustained operating leverage
