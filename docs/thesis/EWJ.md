## Thesis

Long Japan macro bet — new PM, corporate governance reform, weak yen.

Japan is undergoing a structural shift: decades of corporate governance complacency are being unwound. TSE is pressuring companies to improve ROE, unwind cross-shareholdings, and return capital. A new PM is maintaining reform momentum. Weak yen helps exporters and makes Japanese equities cheaper for foreign buyers.

Key pillars:
- **Corporate governance reform:** TSE "name and shame" campaign forcing companies to address price-to-book below 1x. Buybacks and dividend increases accelerating
- **Weak yen tailwind:** Benefits exporters (Toyota, Sony, etc.) and attracts foreign capital
- **New PM mandate:** Political stability supporting reform agenda
- **Valuation:** Japanese equities still cheap relative to US on P/E and P/B basis
- **Buffett signal:** Berkshire's Japan bets (trading houses) brought global attention

## Invalidation Framework

### Early Warning
- Yen starts strengthening meaningfully (below 140/USD)
- Reform momentum stalls — TSE backs off pressure
- Foreign fund flows into Japan reverse
- New PM loses political support

### Invalidation Conditions
- **Yen strengthens sharply** — a move to 130/USD or below would hurt exporters and the foreign buyer thesis
- **Reforms stall** — if corporate governance improvements plateau and companies revert to hoarding cash
- **PM loses mandate** — political instability derails the reform agenda
- **Global risk-off** — Japan is a beta play; severe global recession would hit it hard

### What Would Make Me Add
- Yen weakens further past 155/USD
- Corporate buyback/dividend data shows acceleration
- EWJ pulls back to $80 on temporary macro noise
- Clear evidence reform is structural, not cyclical
