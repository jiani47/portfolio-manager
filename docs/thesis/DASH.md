**Tier:** Starter

**Last Updated:** 2026-03-06

## Thesis

Waiting for more conviction on unit economics to promote to Growth.

DoorDash is the #1 US food delivery platform by market share. The bull case is that delivery becomes a utility — not just food, but groceries, convenience, alcohol, packages. DashPass subscription drives retention and frequency. The question is whether unit economics can sustain profitable growth or if delivery remains a subsidy-dependent business.

Key pillars:
- **Market share dominance:** ~65%+ US food delivery market share. Clear #1 ahead of Uber Eats
- **DashPass flywheel:** Subscription model increases order frequency and retention — similar to Amazon Prime for delivery
- **Category expansion:** Groceries, convenience, alcohol, retail — expanding TAM beyond restaurant delivery
- **International growth:** Wolt acquisition gives European footprint
- **Advertising:** Emerging ad business (sponsored listings) could be high-margin revenue stream

## Invalidation Framework

### Early Warning
- US order growth decelerates below 15%
- Take rate compression from merchant pushback
- DashPass subscriber growth stalls
- International losses widen without clear path to profitability

### Invalidation Conditions
- **Unit economics don't improve** — if contribution margin per order stays flat or declines, the business can't sustain profitability at scale
- **Competition intensifies** — if Uber Eats or new entrants engage in a price war that resets take rates permanently
- **Regulatory risk** — gig worker classification (AB5-style laws) could fundamentally change the cost structure
- **Category expansion fails** — if grocery/convenience delivery doesn't reach food delivery-level frequency

### What Would Make Me Add (promote to Growth)
- Consistent GAAP profitability for 2+ quarters
- Contribution margin per order trending up
- International (Wolt) reaches profitability
- Ad revenue becomes a meaningful percentage of total
- Stock pulls back to $160 on macro
