# Intuit Inc (INTU)

**Tier:** Growth
**Accounts:** Schwab 8819 (35 shares)
**Cost Basis:** $15,087 (~$431.05/share)
**Last Updated:** 2026-03-06

## Thesis

Needs to prove AI-driven growth. Opportunity to gain mid-market share with AI-powered accounting/tax.

Intuit owns TurboTax (#1 consumer tax), QuickBooks (#1 SMB accounting), Credit Karma (fintech), and Mailchimp (marketing). The thesis is that AI transforms these products — Intuit Assist automates bookkeeping, tax prep, and financial decisions. If AI makes QuickBooks smart enough for mid-market companies (currently using NetSuite/Sage), the TAM expands significantly.

Key pillars:
- **SMB lock-in:** QuickBooks is the default for small businesses. High switching costs once your books are in the system
- **Tax monopoly:** TurboTax dominates consumer tax filing. Recurring annual revenue with high margins
- **AI opportunity:** Intuit Assist could automate routine accounting tasks, making QuickBooks viable for larger businesses
- **Multi-product platform:** Tax + accounting + marketing + fintech — cross-sell opportunity
- **Recurring revenue:** ~80% of revenue is subscription/recurring

## Invalidation Framework

### Early Warning
- QuickBooks subscriber growth slows below 10%
- AI features get negative reception or low adoption
- Free tax filing (IRS Direct File) gains meaningful share
- Mid-market push shows no traction after 2+ quarters

### Invalidation Conditions
- **SMB spending contraction** — recession directly hits small business formation and spending on software
- **AI disrupts tax/accounting** — if AI-native startups (or ChatGPT itself) make tax filing and bookkeeping trivially easy, Intuit's moat erodes
- **Mid-market push fails** — if QuickBooks can't move upmarket, growth is capped by SMB TAM
- **IRS Direct File scales** — government-provided free filing would undercut TurboTax

### What Would Make Me Add
- Intuit Assist shows clear product differentiation and adoption metrics
- Mid-market QuickBooks wins (companies with 50-500 employees)
- Stock pulls back to $400 on macro
- Credit Karma monetization accelerates

## Risk Notes

- 35 shares at ~$431 — mid-size position
- High valuation for a mature software company — needs AI growth to justify multiple
- AI is both opportunity and threat — if AI commoditizes bookkeeping, Intuit could be disrupted
- IRS Direct File is a political/regulatory wild card
