## Thesis

BNPL infrastructure play — Affirm is building the rails for point-of-sale lending. Unlike simple pay-in-4 competitors, Affirm underwrites real credit risk with proprietary models and offers flexible terms. The merchant integration (Amazon, Shopify, Walmart) is the moat. The Affirm Card is the TAM expansion story.

**What's working (Q2 FY2026 / Dec 2025 data):**
- **Revenue: $1.12B (+30% YoY).** Beat consensus ($1.06B). Revenue growth is reaccelerating (was mid-20s, now 30%).
- **GMV: $13.8B (+36% YoY).** Above the 30% "add" threshold in the original thesis. Network volume is expanding.
- **Affirm Card is the breakout story.** Card GMV surged 159% YoY to $2.19B. This proves the everyday-spending expansion thesis — AFRM is moving beyond checkout BNPL into general-purpose spending.
- **Active consumers: 25.8M (+23% YoY).** Transactions per consumer: 6.4 (+20%). Users are spending more frequently — engagement is deepening, not just user count growing.
- **Active merchants: 478K (+42% YoY).** Merchant network expanding rapidly. Two-sided network effects intact.
- **Delinquency stable.** 30+ day delinquency at 2-3%. No deterioration despite macro uncertainty. Underwriting models are performing.
- **EPS: $0.37** (beat $0.27 est). Moving toward sustained profitability.

**What the thesis should be honest about:**
- **Stock at $46.75 vs cost basis $73.44 (-36%).** This is the deepest loss in the portfolio by percentage. The fundamentals are the best they've ever been, but the stock has been crushed. The disconnect between business performance and stock price suggests macro fear (recession → credit losses) is dominating.
- **Still a single-product company.** Everything — merchant fees, interest income, card revenue, gain-on-sale — is different flavors of the same thing: consumer installment lending. If BNPL as a category faces regulatory or credit headwinds, there's no diversification.
- **Credit cycle risk is real and unpriced.** 30+ day delinquency at 2-3% is fine today. In a recession, this could spike to 5-8%. AFRM's underwriting is untested in a real downturn (2020 was too brief, 2022 was a growth slowdown not a credit crisis). The entire bear case rests on this.
- **52-week range: $31-$100.** The stock has already been to $31 in the past year. At $47, you're closer to the low than the high. The market is pricing in meaningful credit risk.
- **Path to GAAP profitability still unclear.** Adjusted EPS is positive, but GAAP profitability requires further operating leverage. Stock-based comp is dilutive.
- **Competition intensifying.** Klarna IPO'd, Apple Pay Later exists (though limited), banks launching their own BNPL. The moat is merchant integration depth, but switching costs for merchants aren't infinite.

## Invalidation Framework

### Early Warning (watch quarterly)
- 30+ day delinquency rises above 3.5% for 2+ quarters (currently 2-3%)
- GMV growth decelerates below 25% (currently 36%)
- Card GMV growth decelerates below 100% (currently 159% — very high bar, allow this to moderate)
- Amazon or Shopify add competing BNPL options (Klarna, bank partners)
- Revenue take rate compresses (revenue/GMV declining — would signal margin pressure)
- Active consumer growth below 15% (currently 23%)

### Invalidation Conditions
- **Credit losses spike in downturn** — if charge-off rates exceed 6% in any quarter, underwriting edge is disproven
- **Regulation caps BNPL economics** — CFPB or state regulators cap fees/rates, breaking the unit economics
- **Amazon relationship deteriorates** — if Amazon cuts AFRM or demands materially worse terms
- **Funding market seizes** — if ABS spreads blow out and AFRM can't securitize profitably for 2+ quarters
- **Card adoption stalls** — if Affirm Card GMV growth drops below 50% for 2+ quarters, the TAM expansion thesis dies

### What Would Make Me Add
- Stock below $40 on macro fear (not credit deterioration) — at current levels, this is close
- Affirm Card active users disclosed and exceed 3M
- GAAP profitability achieved for 2+ consecutive quarters
- New major merchant partnership (Costco, Home Depot-tier)
- NOT: "GMV growth reaccelerates above 30%" — this is already happening at 36%. The add trigger should be credit-cycle-proof, not growth-based

## Risk Notes

- **Cost basis $73.44 vs current $46.75 (-36%).** Deepest loss in the portfolio. At 351 shares, $25.8K cost, current value ~$16.4K. You've lost ~$9.4K on this position.
- **Single-product concentration** — everything is consumer installment lending. Unlike SOFI (banking + investing + lending + platform), AFRM has no fallback if BNPL faces headwinds.
- **The fundamental/price disconnect is the key question.** 30% revenue growth, 36% GMV growth, 159% Card growth, stable credit — these are excellent metrics. The stock is at $47. Either the market is wrong (macro fear overdone) or the market is pricing in a credit cycle you can't see yet.
- **Position sizing.** At $16.4K current value, this is ~3% of the portfolio. The loss is painful but not portfolio-threatening. The question is whether to hold through the drawdown for the recovery, or accept the loss.
- **Recession scenario.** If unemployment rises to 5%+, consumer credit losses will spike across all lenders. AFRM's 2-3% delinquency could go to 5-8%. At that point, the stock could revisit $31. This is the bear case, and it's plausible.
