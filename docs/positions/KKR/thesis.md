## Thesis

Private credit market bet + alternative asset management secular growth. KKR is a fee-generating machine. Even if underlying loans go bad, they collect management fees on AUM. The secular trend — capital flowing from public to private markets — is intact.

**What's working (Q4 2025 / FY2025 data):**
- **Fee business is exceptional.** Q4 FRE: $972M (69% margin). Full-year management fees: $4.1B (+24% YoY), evenly split across PE/credit/real assets. This is the core of the thesis — durable, high-margin fees.
- **Record AUM: $744B (+17% YoY).** Fee-paying AUM: $604B. The money keeps flowing in.
- **Record fundraising: $129B in FY2025.** Nearly doubled the pace from two years ago. This is the strongest signal — institutional allocators are increasing alternatives exposure.
- **Deployment strong: $95B invested in 2025.** Across infrastructure, asset-based finance, private credit. They're putting the money to work, not sitting on dry powder.
- **Management "extremely confident" in exceeding 2026 FRE targets.**
- **Insider buying.** Both Co-CEOs bought 175K shares each in Feb 2026 at ~$91. Board buying too.

**What the thesis should be honest about:**
- **Stock at $85.24 vs cost basis $125.88 (-32%).** This is the second-deepest loss in the portfolio. You bought near the top.
- **Only $6 above the $79 price stop.** If KKR closes below $79 (Jan 2024 swing low), the plan is full exit. That's 7% away. You're close to the exit trigger on a -32% loss.
- **FS KKR Credit non-accruals rising: 3.4% (up from 2.9%).** Dividend cut 29% ($0.70 → $0.48/quarter). This is exactly what the early warnings flagged. The credit stress is real.
- **Key distinction:** FS KKR Capital (the credit fund with bad loans) is a managed vehicle, not KKR's balance sheet. KKR the fee machine earns management fees on $744B regardless. But: if credit stress spreads, it affects fundraising (the fee growth engine).
- **The NFP miss (-92K in Feb 2026) is a macro signal.** Healthcare strike was temporary, but the market is pricing in credit cycle risk. If unemployment trends to 4.7%+, the credit thesis breaks.
- **All alt managers are down equally.** Apollo, Blackstone, Carlyle — sector-wide rotation. This isn't KKR-specific. But that doesn't make your -32% less real.
